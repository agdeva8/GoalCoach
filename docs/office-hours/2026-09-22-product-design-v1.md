# Design: GoalCoach v3 — chat-first AI life coach for multi-horizon jugglers

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-09-22
Branch: main
Repo: agdeva8/goalcoachv3
Status: DRAFT
Mode: Startup

## Problem Statement

A person juggling more than one active goal across different time horizons — a 6-month career pivot, a 12-month fitness rebuild, a 3-month side project, an ongoing relationship goal — loses more time to **synthesis** than to capture. Each goal is well-understood on its own. What isn't is *what deserves attention now*, *what's blocking what*, and *what I told myself I'd do last Tuesday at 11pm when I was tired and overcommitted.*

The pain isn't a missing app feature. It's the **continuous cognitive cost of being your own chief of staff for an organization of one** — and the way that cost compounds silently across horizons that don't naturally connect.

Why now: LLMs make chat-first coaching feel less like a toy and more like a real interlocutor. For the first time, the synthesis layer can be a conversation, not an internal monologue.

## Demand Evidence

- **Founder-as-user (real, not hypothetical):** The founder is the first user. Their own multi-horizon juggling is the source of the problem statement, not a guessed persona. Bad product decisions fail on the founder within days.
- **Cohort hypothesis:** Self-directed adults holding more than one active goal across horizons longer than 1 month and shorter than 3 years. They have jobs and lives. They already maintain a notes app of intentions and cannot honestly tell you what they're doing this week and why.
- **Acceptance test (falsifiable):** The coach is right to ship when, given a 30-second "here's my week" prompt, the coach's response is still the right thing a week later. Not impressive — just not embarrassing and not contradicted by the founder's own later recall.

## Status Quo

What people juggling goals actually do, and why each fails at week 2:

1. **Todo apps.** Capture solved, retrieval and prioritization aren't. Single-list by design — a 6-month horizon and a 1-day task look the same. Week-2 failure: important drowns in urgent; manual re-prioritization every Monday; user gives up by week 3.
2. **Note apps (Notion / Obsidian / Apple Notes).** Great for journaling intentions; terrible for surfacing them. Week-2 failure: a strong commitment written on a Tuesday disappears; the user makes the same commitment on a Friday without realizing.
3. **Spreadsheets.** Rigid. Forces a structure the user doesn't fully want. Week-2 failure: starts strong, abandoned by row 4 because the schema doesn't fit a real week.
4. **Slack-DM-yourself / text-message-yourself.** Works for capture. Week-2 failure: zero synthesis; 200 notes to self and no way to ask "what did I promise about X last month?"
5. **Friend / partner / therapist.** Slow cadence, no compounding state, expensive. Week-2 failure: synthesis happens once a week in a session; the rest of the week is back to chaos.
6. **Daily journaling / morning pages.** Emotional, doesn't push back. Week-2 failure: you write what you feel, not what's true. No accountability.
7. **Productivity books / frameworks (Atomic Habits, OKRs).** Generic. Week-2 failure: never specific to your goals.

Common failure across all: **none look across horizons, none remember you said yes on day 1 when you rediscover the same problem on day 14.**

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**User #1 — the founder.** A self-directed IC with a primary work commitment, a fitness/recovery goal, one or two exploratory side projects, and at least one ongoing relationship goal. When asked "what are you working on?" says "depends on the day" and means it.

What gets them unfocused: every other demand that arrives in the same week. The cost of unfocused isn't failing a goal — it's inconsistent half-promises across multiple goals, and weeks quietly lost to drift.

**The wedge (shippable in days, not months):** A single chat thread. The user says *"I have N goals across different time horizons; help me figure out this week."* The coach responds with: (a) a one-paragraph synthesis of what deserves attention and why, (b) three or fewer concrete actions for the next 7 days, (c) one thing the user is currently over-committing to. That's it.

No dashboard. No graphs. No streaks. No calendar integration. No mobile app. A chat. With memory across sessions.

**Zero-setup version:** A textbox at one URL, no signup at v0 (founder is the only user), type, the chat responds, the chat remembers you. Auth is a phase-2 question, not a v0 question.

## Constraints

- **Stack:** none committed. No code, no LLM provider locked, no persistence decision, no hosting decision.
- **Time:** founder-time, evenings and weekends. No team. No deadlines other than the founder's own discipline.
- **Audience:** founder is user #1; cohort is hypothesized but unvalidated.
- **Distribution:** $0 marketing spend at v0–v1; word of mouth only; invite-only until week 8.

## Premises

1. **Chat is the right surface for cross-horizon synthesis, not a dashboard.** A dashboard is for monitoring, not for thinking. The bottleneck is synthesis — a conversation, not a view. *Defend or kill:* build anything other than chat and watch the founder use it less.
2. **Persistence across sessions is required, not optional.** A coach that doesn't remember last week isn't a coach, it's a chatbot with prompts. *Defend or kill:* strip persistence and watch week 2 feel exactly like week 1.
3. **The unclaimed wedge is cross-horizon synthesis, not "AI coach."** "AI coach" is a category incumbents already own with shallow implementations. Cross-horizon synthesis is unclaimed because most tools see one horizon at a time. *Defend or kill:* if a single-horizon tool with better UX beats us in the founder's daily flow, premise 3 is wrong.
4. **Founder-as-user is the right validation loop for v0–v1.** The founder sees the consequence of every design decision within hours or days. Cheap, painful, useful. *Defend or kill:* if the founder stops using v0 within two weeks of building it, the product is wrong.
5. **No mobile, calendar, integrations, or settings at v0.** The narrowest wedge is the chat. *Defend or kill:* if integrations land before v1 ships, scope has crept.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A — Minimal viable chat with file persistence

A web app at one URL: a single textbox, a chat thread, per-user persistence to a small datastore (SQLite for founder-only, Postgres when friends join). The chat is the entire product. No dashboards, no graphs, no settings, no nav.

LLM choice is a tooling decision (any strong long-context generalist). Frontend is one HTML file with a chat component. Backend is a single server function that reads user state, calls the LLM, writes back. No slice architecture, no tool registry, no multi-horizon data model beyond a `goals` table with a horizon field. Auth: v0 = no auth needed (founder only); v1 = a shared password for the friend cohort; v2 = proper OAuth, deferred.

- **Effort:** S — a weekend to a useful v0; a week to v1 with 5 friends.
- **Risk:** Low — small surface area, easy to throw away.
- **Pros:** fastest path to learning whether chat-first is right; can't be over-scoped; the founder can pivot the entire thesis in days.
- **Cons:** may feel under-built when friends try it; no tooling infrastructure; not yet "a product" a stranger would recognize.

### Approach B — Full slice architecture (chat surface, AI tool layer, data model) with proper OAuth and integration scaffolding

Three horizontal slices with explicit contracts between them, a multi-LLM gateway, OAuth, calendar and email integration hooks, a tool registry, a settings layer.

- **Effort:** XL — 4–8 weeks at founder pace.
- **Risk:** Med — well-decomposed, but the cost of architecture is the cost of seeing the founder's real workflow one quarter later than necessary.
- **Pros:** right architecture if the wedge works; supports a multi-horizon data model cleanly; supports future integrations without rewrites.
- **Cons:** founder doesn't get value for weeks; architecture is justified only after the wedge is proven; over-engineering risk before validation.

### Approach C — Voice-first, 2-minute end-of-day spoken debrief

A mobile-web voice interface. The user spends 2 minutes each evening talking aloud. The LLM transcribes, summarizes, asks one synthesis question. The morning session replays yesterday's commitments in 90 seconds.

- **Effort:** M — depends on transcription cost and quality.
- **Risk:** Med — voice UX is harder to get right than text; voice fatigue is real at hour 23 of a long day; transcription accuracy matters more in casual speech.
- **Pros:** friction is even lower than chat; capture happens when context is freshest; mobile-native without building a native app.
- **Cons:** the lowest friction surface is also the lowest surface for the synthesis layer to feel substantive; voice models are noisier than text; doesn't solve the "what should I focus on Monday morning" moment as cleanly as a chat.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach A.** Because the wedge is small enough that anything more than A is a vote-of-no-confidence in the wedge. The hypothesis this version tests is: **does chat-first cross-horizon synthesis create real value for the founder within two weeks of daily use?**

If yes, Approach B becomes the next move. If no, Approach A's small surface makes it cheap to throw away and try Approach C.

Approach A is not a permanent commitment. It is the smallest experiment that can confirm or kill the thesis. Approach B is what we build *after* the thesis is confirmed. Approach C is the experiment we run if Approach A's text-first surface turns out to be friction in a way the founder didn't anticipate.

## Onboarding & Personalization (added 2026-09-23)

The home page is an **area carousel**, not a horizon primer. On first visit, the user is presented with a multi-select grid of life areas (Career, Health, Relationships, Finance, Learning, Fun). The user picks the ones they care about (can change later from settings or via a `/onboarding` revisit) and lands in the chat with that context baked in.

Areas are a **first-class personalization axis**, not a presentation-layer concept:

- **Persistence:** user area preferences persist across sessions and devices, stored in a database column or table scoped to the user. The simplest implementation is a `users.areas TEXT[]` column; a normalized `user_area_preferences` table is fine if we later need per-area metadata.
- **Surfacing in chat:** the chat starts with the user's selected areas in the system prompt as context. The chat input does NOT show inline area chips — the user already picked, and re-picking mid-conversation would be friction.
- **Why not horizons on home:** horizons (Week / Month / Quarter / Year / Multi-year) are a data-model concept for goals, not a primary user-facing axis. Cross-horizon synthesis happens inside the chat via the system prompt, not via up-front selection. The horizon primer (`Primer.tsx`) is removed; the chat starts on a default horizon (the user's most recent, or "this week") and they can change horizon context inside the chat if needed.
- **What changes in implementation:**
  - Home page (`apps/web/app/page.tsx`) renders an `<AreaCarousel />` instead of `<Primer />`.
  - User area selection is persisted on first selection. Skip is allowed (no areas selected = no area context, same as before).
  - `<AreaChips />` (already shipped in `apps/web/components/AreaChips.tsx` as Task 11) is reused for the home carousel. The Task 12 inline mount below the chat input (`apps/web/components/ChatThread.tsx:132`) is reverted.
  - Migration adds `users.areas TEXT[]` (default empty array).

This revision preserves the chat-first premise (Premise 1: chat is the surface, the wedge is the synthesis conversation) and the founder-as-user premise (Premise 4: founder signal drives the design — founder flagged the visual repetition between home and chat, and the visual repetition was the strongest signal we had). The change is in the ENTRY POINT, not the surface.



For the design layer:

- What does the right "answer shape" look like? Short paragraph + 3 actions + 1 warning, or something else?
- How do we distinguish a goal-defining first session from a synthesis-on-day-14 session, in the prompt itself?
- How does the coach surface a contradiction the user hasn't noticed?

For the engineering layer:

- Persistence: SQLite + a single server, or Postgres + a managed instance from day 1?
- Multi-horizon data model: normalized relational (`goals`, `horizons`, `sessions`) or JSONB blob per user?
- How many turns of in-context memory? When does the user-state summary get written back?

For the DX layer:

- What is the first-turn rubric? On a cold user, what does an A-grade response look like?
- How do we measure "still-right-a-week-later"? Self-report? Founder mark-up?
- When do we start instrumenting v0 vs. when do we let founder instinct dominate?

## Success Criteria

- **Founder engagement (week 2):** founder opens the chat 5+ days out of 7. Window: weeks 1–2.
- **Founder engagement (week 4):** founder opens the chat 5+ days out of 7 AND at least one conversation has changed a real decision the founder made that week. Window: weeks 3–4.
- **First-turn quality:** on 7 of 10 cold test scenarios (provided by the founder), the coach's first response passes the founder's own rubric. Window: end of week 2.
- **Friend pull-through (week 8):** 3+ friends have used the chat weekly for two consecutive weeks, unprompted. Window: weeks 6–8.
- **Cohort signal (week 12):** at least one of those friends can articulate (in their own words) what the coach is for, without leaning on the founder's framing.

## Distribution Plan

- **v0 (founder-only):** single chat at one URL, no signup, no marketing.
- **v1 (5 close friends):** shared URL with a single shared password. Manual onboarding from the founder. No landing page beyond a one-paragraph "what is this."
- **v2 (invite codes):** invite codes by request. No public marketing. Distribution by conversation, not by funnel.
- **Later:** public landing only if v2 produces real pull-through from strangers asking to be invited.

## Dependencies

- **LLM API access** — provider and key uncommitted. Pick at kickoff.
- **Persistence** — SQLite or Postgres. Decide at the v0 → v1 move.
- **Hosting** — one server (Render, Fly, or similar) at v1. Decide at v1.
- **Auth** — deferred to v2 at earliest.
- **Audio (only if Approach C is later tried)** — transcription provider, out of scope for v3 v0/v1.

## The Assignment

Before any code:

**Tomorrow morning, write a one-page markdown file titled `acceptance-test.md`.** Ten specific recent moments from your own life where you wished you had this coach. Not hypothetical — actual moments. Each line: a date range, the goal(s) involved, what you said to yourself, what actually happened, and what the coach would have said that was different.

That file is the v3 acceptance test. It will outlast every architecture decision. If we build v3 and it would not have changed the outcome in 7+ of those 10 moments, v3 is wrong — regardless of how clean the code is.

Do not start coding until the file exists.

## What I noticed about how you think

1. **You said "founder is the first user."** Most solo founders hide behind imagined personas for as long as possible. Naming yourself as User #1 commits you to a feedback loop that punishes bad product decisions inside days. The risk: you'll overfit the v0 to your own quirks and call that validation. Watch for the moment a friend's first response surprises you — that's the signal your own loop has missed something.

2. **You named v3, which means prior iterations each taught you something.** The instinct to start clean is correct (drift is real). The risk is treating "clean slate" as a license to re-litigate decisions that already cost you something to learn. Keep a private list of *"things prior iterations taught me that v3 must not relearn the hard way"* — discipline-level, not architecture-level. The drift points you already have in your head are the list.
